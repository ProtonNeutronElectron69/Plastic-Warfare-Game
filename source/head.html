How to Play

Left-drag: select units  •  Double-click: select all of that type on screen  •  Right-click: move / attack / harvest
F + click: attack-move  •  Shift+right-click: queue orders (waypoints)  •  P: patrol between two clicked points  •  H: hold position  •  X: stop
Ctrl+1-9: set group, 1-9: recall  •  Space: select your whole army  •  J: jump to last alert
Q (or the 🏛 HQ button above the panel): select your HQ and open the build menu from anywhere on the map — press again to centre the camera on it, again for your next HQ
Build menu hotkeys: every Construct, Train and Research tile carries a letter in its top-left corner. They're per-building, so the same key means something different depending on what you have selected
Minimap: left-click/drag to look, right-click to order units there  •  ◱ cycles its size  •  a queued unit can be clicked to cancel (full refund)
🧪 Testing Mode (set up before the battle): you command every army in the match. Click an army chip in the top bar or press Tab to switch sides. 👁 Spectate Mode (set up before the battle): four CPU armies fight it out and you just watch — full vision, no orders. The spectator box on the right lists every army's AI profile, resources, supply, live mining rate, its unit / structure / research queues and its army composition. Click a card or press Tab to follow an army; V hides the box. The speed chips (or [ and ]) run the match at 0.75x to 2x — this changes the pace only, never the outcome.

⬢ Plastic is hauled by Dump Trucks from toy piles — stacked building bricks, soldier sprues, and spilled toy-bin scraps. Every plastic pile is marked with a pulsing amber ⬢ above it. A ⛏ over a truck means it's actively mining. ⚡ Electricity is hauled from giant AA batteries, or trickled in by Generators.

Trucks: right-click a pile to harvest it; right-click your HQ to force them to unload right now. Destroyed units leave a salvageable scrap wreck worth % of their plastic cost — send a truck to pick it up.

Build a Barracks for infantry, a Garage for vehicles, a Helipad for choppers. With a production building selected, right-click sets its rally point. Every building (barricades included) must be placed within tiles of your HQ or tiles of one of your Outposts — only the HQ and Outpost themselves are exempt: they can go anywhere you can see (even far from base, but not into the fog of war). Nobody can build within tiles of an enemy HQ, and every structure needs clear between it and any other structure so your units can always drive out of your own base; barricades are exempt from each other, so walls still lay solid. Build an Outpost to push your borders out: it builds Dump Trucks, acts as a resource drop-off so trucks don't have to drive all the way home, and opens a -tile build zone for a forward defensive line. You hold one HQ at a time; if it is destroyed, the 🏛 button above the minimap places a new one at the ordinary price, so losing your headquarters costs you the building and not the match.

Machine Gunner (Barracks): tough, heavy MG. Select him and hit Entrench, then click to aim — he digs in and fires × as fast inside a 100° cone (shown on the ground). Give him a move order to pack up.

Radio Tower (Lab unlock, 1 per army): a static uplink that opens the call-down panel on the right — . Every army gets the Artillery Barrage and the Paradrop; the rest belong to one army each, and you only ever see your own. Napalm is Tan's: it rains fire and a lingering burn across a × area; the Artillery Barrage walks off-map shells through a box, s apart, at damage inside a -tile blast (× against infantry). Those need vision. Paradrop puts elite Paratroopers anywhere, even in fog — SMGs shred infantry, AT grenades crack vehicles, slow HE charges level buildings. Rapid Redeploy is Blue's alone and is the only call-down that creates nobody: select up to of your own infantry, click anywhere on the map even in fog, and they come down under canopies at the far end a moment later. Supply Drop is Green's alone: two crates on canopies, one carrying ⬢ and one carrying ⚡, which lie where they land with no expiry until one of your units walks over them — nobody else can pick them up, and nothing on the board can destroy them. Smokescreen is Gray's alone: a × cloud in which your units and your structures take % less damage for seconds. It is deliberately heavier than the Mortar Squad's Smoke Rounds in every dimension — wider, longer, twice the reduction, and it covers buildings — because it costs a Radio Tower and the shared cooldown where the mortar's costs one loaded tube. Click an ability, then click the map (right-click cancels). One shared cooldown.

Green Army exclusives. Sarge patches himself up at HP/sec once he has been out of contact for seconds, and his On Me! toggle gives allied infantry within tiles +% damage while cutting his own by %. Mortar Squads ramp +% per consecutive shell on one target up to +%, and their Smoke Rounds toggle shells your own men instead: no damage, but a -tile cloud that cuts damage taken by % for seconds. The Radar Tent's Target Uplink gives your whole army + range and + sight for seconds on a -second cooldown. The Command Truck carries no guns at all: its Forward Command is a build zone that travels, opening tiles of buildable ground around it for three structures and no others — — and its Broadcast toggle makes allies within tiles reload % faster while pinning the truck exactly where it stands. The Observation Balloon has the best sight in the game at tiles, drifts slowly, and is the one thing on the board that ordinary weapons cannot touch: only AA missiles can target it at all and they land for % damage, while splash never reaches it. Its High Ground gives every ally inside that vision + range. It is a clock, though — the gas lasts seconds and then it falls, killing everyone aboard, so spend Bail before that to put on the ground beneath it and scuttle the balloon yourself. The Command Post makes your army learn faster within tiles — promotions cost % fewer kills — and its Regroup hands every unit in that radius % of its own maximum health back at once, on a -second cooldown.

Tan Army exclusives. The Flamethrower's Cook-Off leaves everything he touches burning for damage/sec, and his Pressure Valve toggle adds % damage and + range at the price of % of the damage he deals being taken by the man holding the hose. The "Bull" Heavy Tank's ablative plate ignores small-arms fire completely, which is to say cannot hurt it at all, and its Full Throttle toggle adds % speed and crushes enemy infantry and barricades it drives over, but silences both its weapons while it rolls. The Munitions Dump's Scuttle blows the building itself for damage across tiles, and your own units and structures take % of it. The Firebomb Heli sows fire rather than shooting at things: Scorched Earth leaves the ground burning for seconds at damage/sec under everything it hits, so a target that holds its position keeps burning and one that walks away does not. Its Napalm Blast empties the racks — firebombs on random tiles within of the helicopter, damage each, every one leaving seconds of fire at /sec that burns your men and buildings exactly as readily as theirs — on a -second cooldown, the first cooldown ability any unit in the game has carried. The fire is on the ground, so it cannot reach an aircraft: the helicopter that dropped it is safe above it, and so is everything else with rotors. The Foundry gives every vehicle built while it stands +% hull permanently, and its Pour finishes the vehicle at the front of every Garage queue at once for % of each one's plastic on top, on a -second cooldown.

Gray Army exclusives. The Sniper cannot be picked as a target at all until he fires, and drops back out of sight seconds after his last shot — an approach tool above all, since it covers him on the way in rather than through a firefight — and his Called Shot toggle adds % damage but restricts him to enemy infantry for as long as it runs. Rocket Artillery shell-shocks whoever lives through the blast, adding to a survivor's reload on top of whatever it already reads, and its Ripple Fire toggle trades the single shell for a salvo of rockets at % damage each, bursting wider and scattered across the target, paid for with % more reload. The Bunker garrisons infantry who fire from cover and cannot be caught by splash, and its Lockdown buttons them up: the garrison stops firing entirely, but the bunker takes % less damage for seconds, on a -second cooldown. The Choktaw Heli carries two weapons at once and fires both: rocket pods that take armour, aircraft and structures, and a door gun that takes infantry and wildlife, so it never has to choose. It is also the eye of the army — Forward Observer gives every friendly arcing weapon, which is to say the Mortar Squad and the Rocket Artillery, + tiles of reach against anything the Choktaw itself can see, and nothing at all against a target it cannot. Its Paint marks a × area you choose: every enemy unit inside takes +% damage from every source for seconds, on a -second cooldown. The mark rides the men rather than the ground, so it follows them out of the box, and painting a unit that is already lit restarts its clock instead of stacking a second mark. The Heavy Barricade is the ordinary wall at HP, and everything of yours on the tiles around it — units, structures and other walls alike — takes % less damage. That aura stacks: overlapping walls add up, to a ceiling of %, so four of them reach the cap and a fifth is wasted plastic. One in of them buries a mine tiles toward the nearest enemy HQ as it finishes — a mine only your army can see, and one that lets your own men walk over it.

Blue Army exclusives. The Scout Bike is evasive, taking % less damage in any moment he is genuinely moving — which is worth nothing while he sits still trading shots, and that is where most of his damage is taken — and his Flat Out toggle adds % speed and + sight, but holds his fire completely and refuses attack orders while it runs. The Chinook lifts infantry and makes friendly infantry near it hit % harder, and its Air Assault toggle opens the hold so the squad fires out at whatever it can reach — they can be shot back at while they do it, and the aircraft cannot move at all until you close it again. The Wind Turbine's Overdrive runs it at × output for seconds and then produces nothing whatsoever for seconds, on a -second cooldown; across the full cycle that is less electricity than leaving it alone, so it buys the power earlier rather than cheaper. The Signal Runner is weaker and worse-armed than a Grunt on purpose — what you are buying is his radio. Radio Net passively gives friendly infantry within tiles + sight, and his Sprint toggle gives every friendly infantryman within tiles, himself included, +% movement speed — but nobody inside the radius will fire, take an attack order, or so much as acquire a target while it runs. The Forward Pad repairs friendly aircraft within tiles at HP/sec, which is the only aircraft repair in the game, and its Scramble gives every aircraft you own — anywhere on the map, not just the ones on the pad — +% speed for seconds on a -second cooldown.

APC (Garage): unarmed transport carrying 10 infantry — right-click it with infantry selected to load, press U (or the panel button) to unload. It shields itself and nearby allied infantry (−25% damage); if destroyed, the squad bails out losing only 15% health.

Chinook (Blue only, Helipad): the fastest chopper in the game and the biggest transport — infantry, loaded and unloaded exactly like an APC, flown straight over water, walls and minefields. It carries no weapons at all, but allied infantry within 3 tiles of it hit 15% harder (one Chinook only; a second does not stack, though a Munitions Dump does). Over open water it will refuse to unload.

⚔ Counters: what a weapon is firing matters as much as how hard it hits. Every unit has an armor class (infantry, light vehicle, medium armor, heavy armor, aircraft, structure) and every weapon hits each class differently — pick the tool for the job and the same army does two or three times the work. Every build button, research button and selection panel now prints the exact numbers.
Small arms (): fine against men and aircraft, they glance off heavy armor (×0.60) and chew slowly through walls.
Grenades (): shred infantry (×1.20); can barely reach aircraft.
Rockets (): the answer to armor — × medium, × heavy (× for the Bazooka), ×1.30 vs aircraft — but poor against infantry (×0.70), so they need an escort.
Cannon (): the siege line — ×1.40 against any structure, strong against vehicles, mediocre against scattered infantry.
Arcing shells (): the heaviest siege in the game (×1.45 vs structures) and good against infantry clumps, but nearly useless against aircraft (×0.40).
Flame (): melts infantry (×1.35) and does almost nothing to heavy armor (×0.45).
AA missiles (): aircraft only, at × — the zeros against every ground class are the rule, not a rounding: it cannot fire down at all.
Artillery barrage (): the Radio Tower call-down. Full value against armor and structures alike, × against infantry.
The Paratrooper swaps between all three of its munitions automatically: SMG against infantry, AT grenades against vehicles & aircraft, slow HE charges against structures. Open the 📘 Field Manual (setup screen or the top bar) for any unit's exact armor class and its full strong/weak list.

Deathmatch: destroy every enemy structure. If nobody has after minutes, the match is called for whoever has the most base value still standing (completed structures, barricades excluded); ties go to the most kills.
CTF: carry the enemy flag to your stand (your flag must be home). First to captures. Flying units can't carry flags.
👑 King of the Hill: hold the central hill zone with your units. Control it alone (no enemy units inside) to fill your meter; first to s of control wins. You can also still win by wiping out every enemy.

🛡️ Wave Survival: you and any CPU allies start clustered in the middle around a white flag. Survive waves of ants, bees, fire ants, wasps, roaches and mouse mini-bosses that pour in from the map edges. You get 1 minute to build, then a wave lands every 60 seconds. A starter cluster sits by the flag: two plastic piles and two batteries on most maps, while The Desk rings the flag with one plastic-and-battery pair per army plus a spare. Either way you'll need to send trucks out to the map's other nodes to keep building. You lose only if every allied HQ is destroyed.

Research is split across your buildings. Each production building unlocks its own units and its upgrade: train + research at the Barracks (infantry), Garage (vehicles), and Helipad (choppers) — research there runs in parallel with production, one project per building. The 🔬 Research Lab (needs a Barracks) unlocks the other structures (, and your faction building) and their army-wide upgrades: tougher buildings, harder-hitting Guard Towers, more power from Generators/Turbines, a doubled Munitions aura, bigger Bunker garrison, and a faster Lab. Upgraded Barracks/Garage/Helipad turn out units with +20% HP & damage. Build several labs to research structures in parallel. Upgraded buildings show a gold ⬆ badge.

Difficulty (setup screen) only changes how strong the bots are — their economy, army size and damage — never how they play. Profiles (assigned at random) decide each bot's personality: how aggressively it pushes, whether it scouts, harasses or turtles.

Gray's Bunker: right-click it with infantry to garrison (4 max, 6 when upgraded).

⬢ Barricades (build menu): cheap Czech-hedgehog walls, ⬢ each, that block movement. Pick the tool, then click-and-drag to lay a whole line at once (the ghost shows how many you can afford; you can only build where you have vision). Enemies will smash through walls they run up against, so use them to funnel attacks, not to seal yourself in. Scattered dark-gray hedgehogs are neutral and just sit in the way.

⭐ Veterancy: kills promote a unit one rank at a time (gold chevrons) — for infantry, for vehicles, aircraft and everything else. Each rank grants +10% damage and +10% max HP. 💰 Sell: any completed structure except the HQ can be sold back for % of its plastic cost (queued units are refunded in full). 🤝 Teams: armies sharing a team number share vision, never harm each other, and win together.

💥 Landmines: every map is seeded with ~ hidden mines. They're invisible until a ground unit steps on one. A direct hit destroys a tank; a jeep dies one tile away and a grunt about two tiles out. Aircraft fly over them safely. Scout carefully, or push expendable units first.
